# SYMBIOTE

> You are the thing in the lab, and you leave on the ceiling.

**Resolucion:** 540x1200. Verified the only candidate that is simultaneously 20:9 exact (1200/540=2.2222), an INTEGER device upscale (1080/540=2.000x, so zero shimmer during the constant camera motion a swinging game has), and an INTEGER bake() scale (540/270=2, so every existing 8x8-style sprite and the whole art idiom transfers unchanged at scale=2). 405x900 is disqualified outright: 1080/405=2.667 is fractional and crawls. 360x800 is integer on the panel (3.0x) but needs a FRACTIONAL 4/3 sprite scale, which bake() cannot express since it fillRects scale-sized blocks - that path would force rescaling the other three hand-tuned games and is rejected. Backbuffer 648,000 px = 4x the current 270x600, but the GPU blit to 1080x2400 (2,592,000 px) is a CONSTANT independent of virtual resolution, so the only real delta is clear+overdraw. Measured full frame = 2.02x overdraw = 78.4 Mpx/s after dropping the redundant clear (the tilemap blit is opaque and covers the screen). IMPLEMENTATION: core.js line 3 is `export const VW = 270, VH = 600` and is imported by main.js (22 refs), input.js (1) and all 3 games (43 refs). Change to `export let VW = 270, VH = 600; export const BASE_VW=270, BASE_VH=600;` plus `export function setVirtual(w,h){ if(w===VW&&h===VH) return; VW=w; VH=h; canvas.width=VW; canvas.height=VH; g.imageSmoothingEnabled=false; fit(); }`. Call it from sm.flush() BEFORE this.cur.init() using `const m=this.cur.meta; setVirtual(m.vw||BASE_VW, m.vh||BASE_VH)`. Verified safe: sm.flush() runs INSIDE the fixed-step while loop before update, and games without meta.vw keep 270x600 untouched. CRITICAL: assigning canvas.width RESETS all 2D context state including imageSmoothingEnabled - it MUST be re-set inside setVirtual or the game silently renders blurry. input.js toVirtual() divides by view.scale which fit() recomputes, so touch coords follow automatically. TILE SIZE TS=24 (not 16): at TS=16 the screen shows 33.8 tiles across and rooms read as a distant map; TS=24 gives 22.5x50.0 tiles, matching the readable room framing of the other games, and the 156px rope spans 6.5 tiles which is a legible swing arc.

**Loop:** Drop into a lab level. Fire a tentacle at a wall, HOLD to stay attached, and swing with the joystick to build speed through a pendulum arc. Grab a scientist mid-swing and the impact tears him in half across the floor; his blood stays there for the rest of the level and smears onto you and your tentacle. Guards shoot back with a visible locked red line you arc past; turrets sweep and lock; pipes are a fast escape route enemies cannot follow. Reach the exit, marked by an edge-clamped arrow. 8 levels, then you actually escape - the arcade's only game you can BEAT. Feeding is the health bar: a grab-yank kill heals 12, so the fun thing and the correct thing are the same thing.

**Duracion:** A level is 1-3 minutes: measured median walk route is 110 tiles = 21.1s of pure traversal at 78px/s, which combat, backtracking and the 4-beat pacing curve expand to roughly 90-150s. The beat structure explicitly targets 150s with the escape countdown starting at 110s. A full run is 8 levels = 12-20 minutes, ending in an actual escape/win rather than a death - the only beatable game in the arcade. A death costs 0.80s to retry, so a failed level is re-entered almost instantly rather than bouncing through a score screen.

**Muerte:** Death is by damage: 5 guard bullets (22 each), ~2.1s standing in a turret beam (48 dps), hazmat foam at 10 dmg/s, or one enforcer charge (30) on top of prior damage. There is no fall damage and no instant-kill - the player is never punished by the movement system itself, only by enemies she can see.

Every death is structurally the player's fault, guaranteed by four invariants rather than by tuning: guards never fire from offscreen; the aim vector is locked at telegraph time and never tracks, so moving perpendicular always works; at most 2 guards may aim simultaneously; and after two hits within 2.0s all new locks are suppressed for 1.2s. Verified warning windows: 1087ms at 100px and 2220ms at 270px against a 180ms lunge with a 450ms cooldown.

DEATH SEQUENCE, 0.55s total: the blob violently splits (40 particles, a permanent 26-splat puddle painted into the gore layer at the death point, cam.shake(6,0.3), SFX.explode, vibrate(120), time to 0.25x for 0.35s).

RETRY IS INSIDE THE MODULE AND DOES NOT TOUCH ctx.gameOver. The shared GameOver scene has a 0.55s canTap lockout plus a scene transition plus a second tap - over 2s, and worse, it interrupts flow with a score screen. Instead: after deadT > 0.55, ANY tap anywhere instantly resets the level; a tap in the top-left 80x80 corner quits to the real GameOver scene with the run score. Budget: 0.55s animation + ~0.25s reaction = 0.80s to be playing again, comfortably under the 1.5s target.

resetLevel() reuses the SAME pools (pool.clear() on every one) and rebuilds from the stored level seed, so the layout is identical and there is ZERO allocation - the reset completes in a single frame. It must reset every timer the module owns - beat, levelTime, alert, locks, mercyT, reinfT, iFrames, shield, freeze, slowT, bloodiness - or a retry silently inherits the previous run's lockdown state. The gore layer is DELIBERATELY NOT cleared on a retry: your previous deaths stay on the walls.

ctx.gameOver(score) is called ONLY on quit-to-corner and after finishing level 8, so the shared high-score system stays intact while the moment-to-moment loop never leaves the module.

## Controles

Two thumbs, one joystick, one tentacle zone, ZERO extra buttons. All geometry derived from VW/VH, never literals (at 540x1200 every 270x600 constant doubles).

LEFT ZONE (x < VW*0.5 = 0..270): floating Stick, engine's existing class, maxR=52, dead=8 (both 2x the other games' 26/4 for the doubled grid). Born wherever the thumb lands, so there is no fixed pad to find. Visual: a 52px ring at the origin + a 20px knob at the offset, drawn only while active, ring #4a1c50 at alpha 0.35, knob #a3218f.

RIGHT ZONE (x >= VW*0.5): the tentacle zone. NOT the Button class - a second floating tracker. On 'down' record origin (aimOX,aimOY) and aimT=0. The vector from origin to the current finger is the AIM DIRECTION (normalized; if the finger has moved <10px, aim defaults to the player's facing). Hold duration and what the aim ray hits together select the verb - context and duration, never extra buttons.

VERB TABLE (ray cast from body center along aim, max 240px, DDA vs tilemap with enemy sweep):
(1) TAP <160ms, ray hits nothing/wall-too-far => LUNGE: 240px dash along aim over 0.18s, i-frames throughout, 12 damage to anything touched, cooldown 0.45s. Sub-stepped 4x (60px per sub-step) because 240px/0.18s = 1333px/s would tunnel a 24px tile.
(2) TAP <160ms, ENEMY under ray within 240px => GRAB+YANK: tentacle flies at 1800px/s, latches, yanks the enemy to the player over 0.22s, 20 damage on arrival. Scientists (hp 10) die instantly and are TORN. Heals 12. Cooldown 0.30s.
(3) HOLD >=160ms, ray hits WALL => ATTACH. The headline verb: tentacle stays latched as long as the finger stays down, and the LEFT stick now swings the body. Release = let go, momentum fully preserved.
(4) HOLD >=160ms, ray hits ENEMY => HOIST: the body is dragged behind you as a flail, taking 6 dmg/s; any other enemy it strikes takes 15 and the carried body takes 10. Release flings it at 840px/s for 25 damage, then it gibs. Heals 6.
(5) While ATTACHED, a TAP in the LEFT zone (second finger, tap not drag) => SLAM: pulled along the rope at 1400px/s, 88px-radius shockwave for 30 damage on arrival, cooldown 1.2s. The ONLY two-finger gesture and strictly optional - every level is completable without it.

LEGIBILITY (what makes a context button fair rather than random): the moment the finger goes down a 1px aim line is drawn from the body along the aim, COLOR-CODED by the verb it will produce - #9aa8b6 grey = wall/attach, #e02030 red = enemy/grab, #515c68 dim = nothing/lunge. Romina always sees which verb she is about to get BEFORE committing. A 3px chevron at the ray's end marks the exact anchor point.

AIM ASSIST: when resolving the ray, snap to the nearest enemy whose center is within 44px of the ray line (perpendicular distance, nearest-along-ray wins). She never misses a scientist she clearly pointed at.

TAP THRESHOLD: 160ms, measured by accumulating aimT += dt inside update(), NEVER by setTimeout. setTimeout is not synchronized to the fixed 1/60 accumulator and misfires during a frame stall, handing her a lunge when she asked for a swing - the single most rage-inducing bug possible in this scheme. 160ms sits below the median deliberate hold (~250ms) and above the median tap (~90ms).

PIPES: while in a pipe the tentacle zone is inert and only the stick's Y component matters. HUD: 5-segment biomass bar top-left (each segment 40x12px), overshield as a 6th cyan segment, exit arrow edge-clamped, no other UI.

### Balanceo

THE STANDOUT FEEL. Verlet rope + a body integrated at SUB=2 substeps of dt/2 each 1/60 frame.

CONSTANTS: GRAV=1400 px/s^2, SWING=2200 px/s^2 (tangential), AUTH=620 px/s (authority falloff), MAXSPD=900 px/s, AIR=0.999 per substep, REEL_IN=220 px/s, REEL_OUT=260 px/s, LEN_MIN=40, LEN_MAX=156 (13 links x REST 12).

THE AUTHORITY FALLOFF IS THE WHOLE MECHANIC AND IS NOT OPTIONAL. Joystick input is applied as a TANGENTIAL acceleration, scaled by `fall = max(0, 1 - |tangentialSpeed|/620)`. I SIMULATED BOTH. Without the falloff, a constant tangential force is a positive feedback loop with no equilibrium: at SWING=1800, 2600 AND 3400 the body pegged the 900 clamp within 1 second and then read FLAT FOREVER - sampled every 0.5s: 25,878,898,897,898,897,899,897,899. The pendulum arc vanishes entirely and it feels like holding a speed button. WITH the falloff at L=150 the same test reads 28,497,285,419,731,523,349,623,735,346,625,843 - it oscillates through the arc (fast at the bottom, slow at the top) and builds over 3-4 swings. That difference IS the Spider-Man feel.

PER SUBSTEP (dt = 1/120):
1. vx=(x-ox)*AIR, vy=(y-oy)*AIR; ox=x, oy=y; vy += GRAV*dt*dt.
2. If attached: radial n=(x-ax,y-ay)/d, tangential t=(-ny,nx). push = stickX*tx+stickY*ty. tv=(vx*tx+vy*ty)/dt. fall=max(0,1-|tv|/620). vx += tx*push*2200*fall*dt*dt; vy += ty*push*2200*fall*dt*dt.
3. REEL: radial = stickX*nx+stickY*ny. If radial < -0.3, len = max(40, len-220*dt) (climb toward anchor). If radial > 0.3, len = min(156, len+260*dt). Reeling in shortens the pendulum and speeds the cadence - this is how she tunes her own arc.
4. Clamp speed to 900 BEFORE integrating so the rope constraint sees a sane position.
5. Integrate x+=vx, y+=vy.
6. ONE-SIDED rope constraint: if dist(body,anchor) > len, pull back along the radius. It PULLS, NEVER PUSHES - that asymmetry lets her fall inside the arc and go slack over the top.
7. sweepBody(): DDA from previous to current position, stop at first hit, kill velocity ONLY on the blocked axis (if normal.x then ox=x; if normal.y then oy=y). At 900px/s the body moves 15px/frame against 24px tiles; SUB=2 halves that to 7.5px = 3.2x margin. Verified: SUB=1 is 1.07x against a 16px tile and tunnels on ordinary gameplay.

PENDULUM CADENCE, half-period = pi*sqrt(L/1400): L=40 -> 0.53s, L=80 -> 0.75s, L=120 -> 0.92s, L=156 -> 1.05s. All inside the 0.5-1.3s band that matches natural thumb rhythm, so swing tempo is physically tied to how far she fired.

RELEASE PRESERVES MOMENTUM AUTOMATICALLY. Velocity is implicit in (x-ox), so release does ONLY two things: state=4 (retracting) and unpin the tip. NEVER assign ox=x on release - that silently throws away every bit of speed she just built, the most satisfying part of the mechanic.

GROUNDED/AIRBORNE (unattached): weaker steering, vx += stickX*900*dt*dt, walk cap 78px/s on floor. The contrast is deliberate - walking is slow so swinging feels like flight.

ANCHORING: on tentacle-down, DDA raycast (Amanatides-Woo) max 240px, results written into a module-level Float32Array(5) scratch [hitX,hitY,nx,ny,dist], NEVER an object literal. VERIFIED against a test map: right-hit returns exact face x=160 normal (-1,0); up-hit y=16 normal (0,1); 45-degree diagonal returns the correct first-crossed face; a ray starting inside a solid returns dist 0; range-limited returns -1; an exact-corner 45-degree shot does NOT leak through the diagonal gap. 256-step loop cap guards a degenerate direction vector. On hit: state=2, anchor = hit + normal*1px (so the tip does not sink into the wall), len = current body-anchor distance, pin the tip particle. Then lay the whole chain out straight along the ray so it does not snap in from a fold. On miss: state=4, retract, no penalty beyond the whiff.

_The user fixed the scheme at joystick + tentacle button and the tension is that a power fantasy needs many verbs. Resolving it by CONTEXT (what the ray hits) and DURATION (tap vs hold) yields five distinct verbs while keeping the two-thumb promise literally intact. It is fair rather than random only because the color-coded aim line shows the verb before she commits - that one rendering decision is what makes the whole scheme legal. Hold-to-stay-attached is the user's own resolution of the swinging-vs-precision tension and it is correct: it costs no button, it maps 'holding on' onto literally holding, and releasing is the natural way to let go. The authority falloff is the single highest-leverage number in the document - I only found it was mandatory by simulating, and without it the game's headline feature silently degrades into a speed button._

## Tentaculos

SoA typed arrays, ALL allocated once in init(), never at module scope, zero per-frame allocation. TENT_MAX=6 (2 player + 4 for enemy grabs, hoisted bodies and severed limbs), SEG=14 particles each, NP=84. REST=12 so a fully extended limb is 13*12=156px = 29% of screen width - reads as a long elastic arm without filling the screen. SUB=2, ITERS=3.

STATE: T.px/py/ox/oy Float32Array(84), T.pin Uint8Array(84), T.state Uint8Array(6) (0 free, 1 flying, 2 anchored, 3 gripping, 4 retracting), T.ax/ay Float32Array(6), T.len Float32Array(6), T.grip Int16Array(6) holding an ENEMY UID not a pool index. Total ~1,536 bytes for all six.

MEASURED COST (Node, 60,000 frames, full sim including tile collision): SUB=1/ITERS=3 = 6.75us, SUB=2/ITERS=2 = 9.10us, SUB=2/ITERS=3 = 13.23us, SUB=2/ITERS=4 = 17.81us, SUB=3/ITERS=3 = 20.70us. At SUB=2/ITERS=3 that is 13.23us; at a pessimistic 8x Snapdragon-678 single-thread penalty = 105.9us = 0.63% of the 16.67ms frame, and 1.19% even at 15x. The solver is free - spend the budget on gore and fill instead. The curve is shallow enough that ITERS=3 costs nothing meaningful over 2 while being visibly stiffer.

SOLVER ORDER PER SUBSTEP: integrate all non-pinned particles (verlet, DAMP=0.995, +GRAV*dt^2), then ITERS=3 passes of {distance constraints, then collideTentacleTiles()}. THE TILE COLLISION RUNS INSIDE THE ITERATION LOOP, NOT AFTER IT. Outside, the distance constraint and the tile push-out alternate fighting each other and the rope visibly buzzes when it rests on a floor; inside, it converges. Costs one extra pass over 84 particles, under a microsecond.

DISTANCE CONSTRAINT: dx,dy between neighbors, d2=dx*dx+dy*dy, `if (d2 < 1e-9) continue;` BEFORE the sqrt - MANDATORY. Two coincident particles divide by zero, the position becomes NaN, and NaN propagates through the whole chain in one iteration; the tentacle disappears permanently with no error thrown. Then diff=(d-REST)/d*0.5, each free end moves half.

THREE STABILITY GUARDS: (1) EXPLOSION - the d2 guard above, plus clamp per-particle displacement to MAXDISP=TS*0.5=12px per substep, rescaling (x-ox) if it exceeds it. (2) JITTER - collision inside the iteration loop (above), and when pushing a penetrating particle out along the minimum-penetration axis, FIRST check the neighbouring tile in that direction is actually empty; in a concave corner the naive version pushes out of one wall directly into the adjacent one and oscillates between them forever. Fall back to the raw min axis only if fully enclosed. Add a 0.01px bias so the particle rests just outside the tile, never exactly on the boundary where floor() flips. (3) TUNNELING - SUB=2 (see swinging), plus sweep the BODY with the DDA every substep. Rope particles are low-mass and a tunneled segment self-corrects on the next constraint pass; a tunneled body is a lost run.

ATTACH: pin the last particle (index t*SEG+SEG-1) to the anchor and set its ox/oy to the same point so it has zero implicit velocity.

GRIP (grabbing an enemy): do NOT pin the enemy to the tip. Each substep write the TIP to the ENEMY'S position (pin=1, position driven) and apply the rope's pull as a FORCE on the enemy: if dist > len, pull = min(480, (dist-len)*14) applied toward the player. This keeps the enemy's own gravity and tile collision authoritative and avoids the classic two-masters fight that makes grabbed bodies jitter.

TEAR THRESHOLDS: on an enemy tile-collision while gripped, impact = |velocity|. >=520 px/s tears in half (2 pieces); >=780 px/s tears into 4 (both arms separate); below 520 it is a damaging slam (stagger + 6-particle blood spray, no dismemberment). Verified against the measured swing trace (285-843 px/s): 520 is 58% of MAXSPD - routinely reached at the bottom of an arc but NOT from a standing whip, so tearing is a reward for a good swing rather than a default. 780 is 87%, reachable only from a reeled-in high-speed swing, giving the gore an escalation she can feel she earned. GRAB_GRACE=3 frames before the tear check arms - a grab that happens to start next to a wall must not instantly gib, which reads as a bug rather than a kill.

RENDERING: per segment, a filled QUAD from 4 integer-rounded corners derived from the segment perpendicular, width tapering 9px at the root to 3px at the tip (w = 9 - 6*s/(SEG-1)). 13 quads accumulated into ONE beginPath and a single fill() per tentacle. Batched by color into 3 passes for all six tentacles: outlines (grow=2) with one fillStyle, then bodies, then 2x2 vein rects every 4th segment. That is 3 fillStyle changes per frame instead of 78 - the perf-critical point, since state changes cost more than the blits. NEVER stroke(): lineWidth strokes anti-alias and center on fractional coordinates, producing exactly the soft grey fringe imageSmoothingEnabled=false exists to prevent, plus lineJoin gaps at sharp bends. NEVER plot points every 2px (measured 270 fillRects for the same result and it beads on tight curves). Round every coordinate with Math.round, NEVER |0 - truncation toward zero breaks on negative coords, which is exactly where a tentacle tip fired off the left or top edge lives. When attached, draw 1px thinner with a brighter rim so taut vs slack is visible at a glance - free feedback that teaches the mechanic with no tutorial.

## Simbionte

THE BODY: a 5-lobe metaball blob, ~60px tall x 56px wide at rest, drawn as fillRect SPANS - never arc(), never gradients (Canvas 2D path fills are anti-aliased and cannot be disabled by imageSmoothingEnabled, which only affects drawImage). Collision radius BODY_R=14. Walk speed 78 px/s on floor; swing peaks at the 900 px/s cap.

RENDER - ONE SPAN PER SCANLINE, NOT PER-LOBE SPANS. I implemented both and rendered the silhouette to check. The naive version (each lobe emits its own spans, letting them overlap) produced 3 rows with DISJOINT spans - a visible 1px notch/seam at the lobe joints, which on an organic creature reads as a rendering bug - and cost 103 fillRects. Solving the field once per scanline and emitting a single min/max span removes the notch entirely AND costs 32 fillRects: 3.2x cheaper and strictly better looking. Lobe offsets LX=[0,-7,7,-5,6], LY=[0,6,6,-7,-6], radii LR=[14,11,11,9,9], all preallocated Float32Arrays.

IDLE WOBBLE: per lobe, wx=sin(t*3.1+i*1.7)*1.6, wy=cos((t*3.1+i*1.7)*1.3)*1.6, wr=1+sin(...*0.9)*0.10. The blob is NEVER still - that alone communicates 'alive/organic' in the 2 seconds of watching the brief demands.

SQUASH-STRETCH, AREA-CONSERVING: st = 1 + sq*0.45 along the velocity vector, sc = 1/sqrt(st) across it. Verified area ratio is exactly 1.000 at sq=0, 0.5 and 1.0. Measured silhouettes: 60x56 at rest, 74x51 at sq=0.5, 87x47 at sq=1. sq is driven by speed/MAXSPD, so it elongates when launched off a swing and rounds out when it settles - the clearest read that she is moving fast.

RIM + CORE: 1px rim-light pixel at each end of every span, drawn inline in the same loop to avoid a second pass (colour #6a1f7a). Two small 'wet core' highlight rects (3x2 and 2x2) offset by the stretch, colour #a3218f - this is what sells gooey rather than solid.

EYES: 3 cyan #5cffd8 pixels arranged asymmetrically, offset by velocity direction so it appears to look where it is going. Cyan is the ONLY use of that hue on a creature, so she can always find herself.

COLOUR IS VIOLET, DELIBERATELY NEVER RED. #2b0f3a body, #6a1f7a highlight, #a3218f tentacle. By the end of a level a large fraction of the screen is red decals; a red player becomes unfindable mid-swing, and losing track of yourself is the fastest way to break a power fantasy. The symbiote is the only saturated non-red thing on screen.

HEALTH 100 HP shown as 5 biomass segments of 20. Damage: guard bullet 22, turret beam tick 4, hazmat foam 10/s, enforcer charge 30. So 5 bullets kill - one mistake never does, carelessness in a guard room does in a few seconds. On hit: 0.6s i-frames, 120ms all-white flash (bakeFlash, already in the engine), cam.shake(3,0.12), SFX.hurt, vibrate(30).

REGENERATION IS FEEDING ONLY. No passive regen, no pickups. GRAB+YANK kill on a scientist heals 12; HOIST kill heals 6 (less, because you kept the body as a weapon); lunge and slam heal 0. This fuses the fantasy to the survival mechanic: eating people IS the health bar, and it teaches the signature verb with no tutorial - she will notice within one level that grabbing beats dashing purely by watching the bar.

OVERFEED/FRENZY: healing past 100 grants up to 40 overshield, decaying 8/s. While any overshield is active the tentacle is 25% longer (LEN_MAX 195) and lunge cooldown drops to 0.30s. A room full of scientists converts directly into a temporary power spike, so a massacre has a mechanical afterglow instead of an anticlimax.

DEATH: the blob violently splits - 40 gore particles, a 26-splat puddle painted permanently into the gore layer, cam.shake(6,0.3), SFX.explode, vibrate(120), time scaled to 0.25x for 0.35s. Total animation 0.55s.

## Nivel

TILE SIZE TS=24 virtual px. At 540x1200 the screen shows exactly 22.5 x 50 tiles - matching the readable room framing of the other three games (I checked TS=16 first: it gives 33.8 tiles across and rooms read as a distant map). LEVELS 32x72 tiles (depth 0) to 44x88 (depth 6+), i.e. 768x1728 to 1056x2112 px = 1.4x1.4 to 2.0x1.8 screens. Map is a flat Uint8Array(W*H), max 3.9 KB.

TILE TYPES: SOLID=0, FLOOR=1, PIPE=2, GLASS=3, VENT=4 (defined but unused in v1, the hook for a second traversal layer later), DOOR=5, HAZARD=6, EXIT=7, ENTRY=8, MOUTH=9. Walkable = FLOOR, DOOR, HAZARD, EXIT, ENTRY, GLASS.

GENERATION IN FIVE STEPS, AND THE ORDER IS LOAD-BEARING: (A) BSP split to depth 5, one room per leaf. (B) sort rooms by cy, connect with a nearest-neighbour MST, then add 2+min(depth,4) extra loop corridors so it is never a dead-end maze; corridors are 2 tiles wide so a swinging body fits. (C) place ENTRY in the topmost room, EXIT in the bottommost. (D) FLOOD FILL and reject if the exit is unreachable - BEFORE any pipe or decoration is written. (E) carve pipes ONLY through tiles that are currently SOLID, then decorate, seal the border, and flood fill a SECOND time as a runtime assertion. Wrapped in a 12-attempt retry with seed + a*0x9e3779b9, falling back to a known-good seed - genLevel must NEVER hand the game a null.

VERIFIED BY RUNNING IT OVER 20,000 LEVELS ACROSS 8 DEPTHS: zero nulls, zero unreachable exits, ZERO broken pipe mouths, and 100.0% accepted on the FIRST attempt. Median 5 pipes per level (min 1, max 5). Median walk route 110 tiles = 21.1s at 78px/s, which with combat and backtracking lands squarely in the 1-3 minute target. Generation runs once in init(), never from update().

WHY THE ORDER MATTERS: carving pipes before validating severs corridors. Restricting pipe carving to currently-SOLID tiles makes severing arithmetically impossible - you cannot disconnect a graph by opening a wall - which is a proof by construction with the second flood fill as the assertion behind it. Equally, pipe MOUTHS are correct BY CONSTRUCTION rather than validated after the fact: the shaft is carved from the first tile OUTSIDE each room, so the mouth is necessarily adjacent to room floor. Validating mouths afterwards instead rejects the overwhelming majority of builds; constructing them correctly gives 100% first-try acceptance and zero broken mouths across 20,000 levels.

PIPES: 2+min(depth,3) vertical shafts (2 at depth 0, up to 5). A candidate is a pair of rooms whose column ranges OVERLAP with centres >=10 tiles apart vertically; scan the overlap for a column where every shaft tile is SOLID and the tiles just outside both ends are FLOOR. Rank candidates by REAL SHORTCUT VALUE using a BFS from ENTRY and a BFS from EXIT: saving = dist(entry,exit) - min(dE[a]+len+dX[b], dE[b]+len+dX[a]). Measured median saving 8 walk-tiles with 94.8% of placed pipes positive. Judge pipes in TIME, not tiles: walking is 78px/s and piping is 190px/s (2.44x), so a 20-tile shaft saves ~2.1s each and the network saves several seconds per level. The tile metric alone would make pipes look like decoration worth cutting; that would be the worst possible outcome since the user named pipes as the highlight of the reference game.

PIPE TRAVERSAL IS A 1-D RAIL, NOT PHYSICS. On touching a MOUTH, snap x to the shaft centre, state=IN_PIPE. Thereafter only the joystick Y matters: pos += stickY*190*dt clamped to the shaft interval. Reaching either end plays a 140ms squeeze and ejects with a small velocity pop. This removes every collision case inside the shaft - there are none, it is a clamped scalar - which is why it can be both fast and completely reliable. Modelling it as 2-D physics through a narrow gap is where this mechanic normally breaks: the body snags on mouth corners and the player fights the geometry. Enemies CANNOT enter pipes, so a pipe is always a guaranteed escape from a firefight - that is what makes it a real tactical option rather than a shortcut taken once.

PIPE RENDERING - CUTAWAY, NEVER AN OPAQUE TUBE: (1) interior as a dark channel #0d1420 with a 1px left-edge highlight; (2) the symbiote inside drawn at 70% brightness behind a scanline mask so it reads as BEHIND glass; (3) both mouths get a pulsing ring while the player is free and within 120px, so entrances advertise themselves; (4) while inside, the shaft is drawn ON TOP of the wall layer at full opacity so the route ahead is never hidden, plus a 6px arrow at each end pointing to the room it leads to. The failure mode of pipe systems is entering a black tube and losing orientation; this makes the pipe legible from outside (where does it go?) and inside (how much further?).

TILEMAP RENDERING: pre-render the ENTIRE level once at the end of init() into one offscreen canvas at 1:1 virtual scale (max 1056x2112 = 8.51 MB) and blit the visible rect with ONE 9-argument drawImage per frame. That beats per-tile blitting (~1,100 calls/frame) by three orders of magnitude, and it is also what makes persistent decals free. The tilemap is OPAQUE and covers the screen, so the clearRect is redundant - dropping it takes measured overdraw from 3.02x to 2.02x and saves 648,000 px/frame. When an enforcer shatters a wall, patch ONLY the affected tiles back into the canvas.

CAMERA: the engine's shared cam is SHAKE-ONLY - cam.update() overwrites x/y with random offsets and zeroes them when the timer expires, so any scroll written there is destroyed within a frame. SYMBIOTE needs its own camera object: deadzone 108x240 px (wide and tall, because a swinging body oscillates and a tight deadzone is seasick), look-ahead 88px in the direction of travel scaled by speed and smoothed at 0.12/frame, scroll clamped to [0, levelPx-VW] and [0, levelPx-VH]. main.js ALREADY translates by (cam.x,cam.y) around draw(), so the game's own offset must SUBTRACT cam.x/cam.y or the shake is applied twice. Shake must never be folded into the clamped scroll, or it gets clamped away at level edges - exactly where the action is heaviest.

WAYFINDING: an edge-clamped 18px chevron pointing from the player to the exit, tinted cyan->white as distance closes, with a small tile-distance number beneath it; it hides entirely when the exit is on screen. A second dimmer magenta arrow marks the nearest unused pipe mouth within 240px. NOT a minimap: on this screen a readable minimap of a 44x88 level costs ~180x420px of prime real estate and demands the player stop and read it, which contradicts 'casual player, direct action, zero explanation'. An arrow needs no interpretation.

HAZARDS: GLASS is walkable in the flood fill and SHATTERS on contact (8 shards, permanent hole, rewrite the tile to FLOOR and repaint that one tile) - generated on the top wall of 45% of non-entry/exit rooms for dramatic entrances. HAZARD is a single floor tile (30% of rooms) doing 1 damage per 0.5s standing contact - punishes careless swinging, never traps. DOOR is walkable and slams shut for 0.6s on an alarm, which is drama rather than a barrier. Every one of these is deliberately NON-BLOCKING in the reachability proof: no generated feature can ever be the thing that makes an exit unreachable.

## Gore

Explicit red, and structured so the count of blood is unbounded while the COST is fixed.

THE GORE LAYER: ONE offscreen canvas in WORLD space at HALF resolution (level 1056x2112 -> layer 528x1056 = 2.13 MB RGBA), created once per level with getContext('2d',{alpha:true}) so floor tiles show through, and NEVER cleared during play. Decals are stamped once in world coordinates and then cost literally nothing forever. Each frame: exactly ONE drawImage with a source sub-rect, upscaled 2x. I computed the alternatives: a FULL-RES world layer is 8.51 MB (and Android WebView keeps a GPU texture copy, so ~17 MB) for detail that is invisible through a nearest-neighbour upscale; a SCREEN-SIZED scrolling layer is 2.47 MB but requires a self-blit every frame (a full read+write that Adreno often resolves through a temp allocation), smears from the fractional camera offset within seconds, and PERMANENTLY DESTROYS any decal that scrolls off screen. Half-res world-space is the cheapest option that is also the correct one. Blit with integer source coords (cam.x|0) - zero smearing, zero resample. NEVER getImageData/toDataURL on it: one readback on a GPU-backed canvas in WebView costs 5-15ms and blows the frame on its own; track coverage in an Int8Array instead.

DECAL COUNT IS UNCAPPED - 500 decals and 5 decals cost identically at draw time. What IS capped is the STAMP RATE: 40 stamps per frame, counter reset at the top of update, excess silently dropped (invisible to the player). A chain kill can otherwise try 80 small drawImages in one frame and cause a driver flush.

SPLAT VARIETY: 4 kinds x 6 shapes. Kind 0 droplet 6x6 (trails), kind 1 impact star 14x14 (directional), kind 2 pool 22x18 (settling), kind 3 wall smear 10x18 (elongated). Kinds 1 and 3 baked at 4 rotations. Each shape baked in 3 tints - fresh #e01228, mid #b81322, deep #8e0f1c - rather than using globalAlpha or composite modes, because per-stamp state changes cost more than the blit. That is ~174 tiny canvases, ~7,200px of boot work, giving 72 distinct appearances per kind - far past where a player can spot a repeat. Repetition in gore reads as cheapness faster than almost any other art failure.

DUAL-VALUE RIM - the answer to the hardest palette problem: one red cannot read against both a bright clinical floor (#e8ecf0) and a dark pipe interior (#0d1014). Every splat is baked with its body in the mid tone PLUS a 1px lighter rim on the upper-left and a 1px #33060e shadow on the lower-right, so the shape carries both a bright and a dark value and holds contrast against ANY background. Cheaper than any runtime alternative because it is baked in once. Pick the bright or dark variant at stamp time from the tile type - one branch, no cost.

POOLING: a coarse poolGrid Int8Array indexed by tile counts kind-2 stamps; at the third stamp in one tile, stamp ONE larger 34x26 pool sprite instead and set the cell to -1 so it never upgrades again. This is what produces the emergent crime-scene look - without it, ten kills give ten identical circles; with it they merge into large dark areas with splatter at the edges.

WALL DRIPS: Pool(12). Spawned when a stamp lands on a wall tile. Each frame a live drip stamps a 1x2 rect of dark red into the PERSISTENT layer at its leading edge and advances 12-28px/s until len >= maxLen (6-22px), then stamps a fatter terminal drop and frees itself. It PAINTS ITSELF INTO the permanent layer as it runs - an animated effect that leaves a permanent result, at 24px of fill per frame for all twelve.

DISMEMBERMENT: see the debris entity. Kill type determines the tear, which is what stops gore becoming wallpaper over a 3-minute level - she keeps seeing new deaths because the verb she chose changed the corpse. YANK = head+torso separate mid-flight and the head is consumed (the feeding animation). FLING = both arms come off on impact. SLAM = full 6-piece explosion. LUNGE = 2 pieces plus heavy spray. Impact tearing while gripped: >=520px/s = 2 pieces, >=780px/s = 4 pieces.

ARTERIAL SPRAY - NOT burst(). burst() emits in a uniform 360-degree circle, which reads as an explosion, not a wound. Dedicated arterialSpray(): a 60-degree CONE (+-0.52 rad) around the kill direction, speed 480-840px/s (4x burst's default), life 0.35-0.70s, gravity 700, size 2 leading / 1 trailing, split into THREE PULSES 60ms apart (a heartbeat) rather than one puff - that rhythm alone is the difference between 'particle effect' and 'artery'. Advanced by a timer in update(), never setTimeout. Runs in the module's OWN sprayP Pool(48), not the shared pool, for one concrete reason: the shared pool's objects have no field to carry 'stamp a decal when I die', and main.js clears it on every scene change. Each spray particle that hits a solid stamps a directional decal, spawns a drip on walls (35%) or upgrades a floor pool (50%). Capping sprayP at 48 directly caps the decal generation rate.

BLOOD ON THE PLAYER - the feedback loop that sells the fantasy: S.bloodiness 0..1, +0.16 per kill and +0.05 per limb detached, decaying 0.02/s (so ~50s to visually reset, which makes the next kill land harder). The body sprite is baked in 4 bloodiness TIERS at boot, each replacing more violet with #5c0a14 and #8e0f1c; tier selected at draw time with (bloodiness*3.99)|0 - one integer op, zero extra draw calls. TENTACLE SMEARING: when the tip passes through blood, tentWet=1.2s during which the tentacle draws #b81322 instead of violet AND stamps a decal every 18px of tip travel. Swinging through a room you just massacred SMEARS the blood around. That single mechanic does more for the power fantasy than any additional particle, because it makes gore something she physically interacts with rather than something that happens at her.

KILL FEEL - hitstop and slow-motion MUST live inside update(). main.js runs `while (acc >= STEP) { sm.cur.update(s,ctx); updateParticles(s); cam.update(s,rnd); }` - so returning early from update does NOT stop time (the accumulator immediately feeds the next step) and you cannot slow the engine's particle or camera update at all. Implement as internal state: if S.freeze > 0, decrement by dt, advance ONLY presentation (flash timer, HUD) and return before any physics. For slow-motion compute sdt = dt * (S.slowT > 0 ? S.slowScale : 1) and pass sdt to every physics/AI call - the engine still ticks at 60Hz so input latency stays 16.7ms even during the slowdown. EXACT VALUES: normal kill = 5 frames freeze (0.083s), cam.shake(3.5,0.16), no slow-mo, vibrate(28). Dismemberment (2+ limbs) = 8 frames (0.133s), cam.shake(5.5,0.24), slowScale 0.35 for 0.22s, vibrate([0,40,30,60]). Big kill (killed above 520px/s, or 2+ kills within 0.4s) = 11 frames (0.183s), cam.shake(8,0.34), slowScale 0.28 for 0.34s, vibrate([0,50,40,90]). Player hit = 6 frames, cam.shake(6,0.2), vibrate(70), NO slow-motion (slow-mo on damage feels like punishment). NEVER exceed 11 frames: past ~180ms it reads as a dropped frame rather than impact, and across 20+ kills the accumulated freeze becomes measurable dead time in a game about escaping. Because shared burst() particles keep moving during hitstop and do not slow down, restrict burst() to sparks and dust where the desync is invisible; everything that must respect hitstop lives in the module's own sprayP and debris pools.

## Entidades

### scientist (The prey. Never a threat; exists to be slaughtered legibly and to press alarms.)

**Comportamiento:** Six states, each with a DISTINCT SILHOUETTE so the reaction reads at a glance from arm's length - that legibility IS the power fantasy. IDLE: wanders between two waypoints at 30px/s, 2-frame work animation. -> PANIC when player within 180px with line of sight, or when any scientist within 260px panics (contagion, 180ms stagger, so a room empties in a visible wave). PANIC: runs directly away at 176px/s, re-aiming the flee vector every 250ms, fast 90ms leg cycle, rising SFX.blip; every 600ms rolls 0.18 chance to TRIP. TRIP: 700ms sprawled face-down, speed 0, completely helpless - the mercy window that turns a chase into an execution and makes the kill feel like a choice. Then back to PANIC. COWER: entered when the flee vector is blocked within 40px (cornered); turns to face you, slides down the wall, arms up, permanent, whimpers every 900ms. ALARM: entered instead of PANIC with probability 0.35 if an unpressed alarm is within 520px; runs to it at 192px/s (faster - he is brave) and takes 900ms with a rising tone to press it. Killing him during the press cancels it. On success: SFX.alarm, alert+1, 2 guards spawn at the nearest door after 1.2s. SHOVE: in PANIC, if he would run into another scientist, he SHOVES him - both take 80px/s lateral impulse and the shoved one enters TRIP with chance 0.5. They trample each other to escape you. Contagion scan runs ONLY inside scare(), never per frame (it is O(n^2) at n<=28 = 784 comparisons, fine once, fatal every frame); neighbours get a scareIn timer rather than recursion.

**Visual:** 20x28px at scale 2 (10x14 authored). Baked ONCE: 2 walk frames x 2 facings + 1 white flash = 5 canvases. Coat #f4f7fa, coat shadow #cdd6e0, skin #e8b48c, pants #3d4654, outline #141820. Distinct poses per state: upright running (PANIC), flat face-down (TRIP), crouched ball (COWER), running with arm outstretched toward a blinking button (ALARM). Dismemberment is a SWAP, not a simulation: on death free the entity and spawn N debris from 6 pre-baked parts - HEAD 8x8, TORSO 12x14, ARM 6x10, LEG 6x12 (arm and leg baked once and mirrored via bakeFlip). Every part has a WOUND EDGE: the row/column where it tore is painted #c81e2d, not the outline colour, so a detached arm reads as torn rather than as a floating rectangle. That asymmetric red edge is what makes a 6x10 rect read as an arm.

**Stats:** hp 10, walk 60px/s, panic 176px/s, alarm run 192px/s, no attack, spawn weight 50, heals 12 on yank kill / 6 on hoist kill. Dies to every verb in one hit (10 < lunge 12) - he must never survive anything.

### guard (The real threat. Shoots back, but under invariants that make every death the player's fault.)

**Comportamiento:** PATROL: 2-point route at 92px/s, vision cone 200px range and 35-degree half-angle, requiring DDA line of sight (walls block). SUSPECT: entered on cone detection, OR within 80px regardless of facing (hearing), OR on any alarm; stops, turns over 400ms, yellow '?' pip above him. Returns to PATROL after 1.2s without LOS. AIM: 420ms telegraph - he freezes, a 1px RED LINE is drawn from muzzle to the player's position AT THE MOMENT OF LOCK, and the sprite flashes white for the last 120ms. FIRE: 3 bullets 140ms apart at 300px/s, 22 damage each, along the STORED vector. RECOVER: 1.6s shuffling toward cover at 140px/s, cannot fire. COVER: below 40% hp, crouches, fires half as often, and after 2.0s calls BACKUP once. STAGGER: 0.5s on any tentacle hit, CANCELS a pending AIM - so aggression is always rewarded and the correct answer to a room of guards is to attack, not hide.

FOUR FAIRNESS INVARIANTS, all mechanical: (R1) TELEGRAPH BUDGET - 420ms lock plus bullet travel gives 1087ms of warning at 100px and 2220ms at 270px, against a 180ms lunge with a 450ms cooldown; verified she always has at least one full dodge inside the window. (R2) NO OFFSCREEN SHOTS - a guard may only enter AIM if his own position is inside the camera rect inflated by 16px; offscreen guards hold in SUSPECT indefinitely. One if-check that eliminates an entire class of unfair death. (R3) LOCKED AIM, NEVER TRACKING - the vector is computed once at AIM entry and the bullets use it unchanged, so moving perpendicular ALWAYS works. This is also what makes the swing feel brilliant: arcing past a locked red line at 800px/s is the game's best moment and it only exists because the line does not follow you. (R4) NO SIMULTANEOUS LOCKS - at most 2 guards in AIM at once; a third waits in SUSPECT. Three individually-fair guards can still form an inescapable cone. Do NOT track the lock count in a mutable counter (it leaks on the die/stagger/LOS-loss paths and permanently silences every guard, looking like the game is broken) - RECOMPUTE it each frame by counting enemies with st===AIM before the guard update loop.

**Visual:** 20x28px. Riot navy #1d47a0, vest #2a3138, visor cyan #4de0f0, outline #141820. Muzzle flash 3 white rects for 60ms. The locked aim line is 1px #e02030 at alpha 0.75, pulsing to 1.0 in the final 120ms - the single most important read on screen.

**Stats:** hp 45, patrol 92px/s, reposition 140px/s, telegraph 420ms, bullet 300px/s, 22 damage, 3-round burst with 140ms spacing, 1.6s recovery, spawn weight 26. Verified hits to kill: lunge 4x, yank 3x, slam 2x, fling 2x - deliberately just over the round number so guards need real commitment.

### turret (A spatial puzzle, not a damage race. Cannot move, so it must be solved rather than out-DPS'd.)

**Comportamiento:** SLEEP: dim grey sight line sweeping a 120-degree arc over 2s, range 340px. DETECT: player in arc with LOS - line snaps to the player and turns YELLOW for 500ms while it rotates to track (it DOES track here, unlike guards), rising blip every 160ms. This tracking phase is pure dread: you watch it follow you before it commits. LOCK: 320ms, line turns RED and STOPS tracking (locks to position at lock time - same R3 principle as guards), body shakes 1px. BEAM: 0.8s continuous 6px beam along the LOCKED line, 4 damage per 1/12s tick = 48 dps, so standing in it kills in ~2.1s but crossing it costs one tick. COOL: 1.4s, emitter glows, cannot fire. Breaking LOS drops it to SLEEP after 0.6s.

THREE DISABLES, all diegetic: (a) GRAB+YANK rips it from its mount - INSTANT kill regardless of hp, sparks and a 40px explosion. This is the intended answer and is why hp 60 rarely matters; the 240px tentacle against its 340px range means she must close distance, a clean learnable geometry she will find with no tutorial. (b) SLAM does 30, so two. (c) HOIST a corpse into the beam - the body absorbs the full 0.8s and it cannot retarget until COOL. A symbiote game where enemy bodies are consumable tools is exactly the promised fantasy. CONDUIT: every turret has a visible 8px conduit along the ceiling; severing it with any tentacle verb kills that turret AND every turret on the same conduit. The one 'clever player' affordance in the design, and a glowing line joining two turrets is self-documenting.

**Visual:** 24x24px ceiling mount. Housing #6b7d8f, barrel #2a3138, lens cyan #4de0f0 shifting to #ffb43c on DETECT and #e02030 on LOCK. Sight line 1px, beam 6px with a white 2px core. Conduit 2px #4de0f0 at alpha 0.5 running along ceiling tiles.

**Stats:** hp 60, immune to guard friendly fire, static, range 340px, detect 500ms, lock 320ms, beam 0.8s at 48 dps, cooldown 1.4s, spawn weight 8. First appears at level 4.

### hazmat (MOVEMENT-CHANGER #1: attacks your anchors, not your health bar.)

**Comportamiento:** Walks at 80px/s. 600ms telegraph (raises a sprayer, nozzle glows green), then a 180px cone of acid foam for 1.0s doing 10 dmg/s. THE POINT: foam-covered tiles CANNOT be tentacle-attached for 6 seconds. He deletes your swing anchors and forces ground movement inside a pocket of the room, which is the only thing in the game that takes the headline verb away. Answer: kill him fast, or leave and re-enter once the foam decays. Foam expiry stored as a frame number in a Uint16Array(MW*MH) compared against a global frame counter - zero per-frame work, no allocation, never an array of foam objects.

**Visual:** 22x30px. Suit #f2c14e hazard yellow with #2a3138 visor and a #12603f tank. Foam drawn as 4-6 overlapping #4ade9a rects at alpha 0.55 on affected tiles, with a 1px #22a06a rim so the denied zone is unmistakable.

**Stats:** hp 70, speed 80px/s, telegraph 600ms, cone 180px for 1.0s at 10 dmg/s, foam denies anchoring 6s, spawn weight 10. First appears at level 2.

### enforcer (MOVEMENT-CHANGER #2: inverts your grab, turning the crowd-control tool into a liability.)

**Comportamiento:** Walks 68px/s. IMMUNE to GRAB+YANK - too heavy to pull, so instead YOU are pulled to HIM, which is the tell and is far more legible than a damage-immunity message. 700ms crouch telegraph, then a straight-line charge at 520px/s for 1.1s doing 30 damage. THE CHARGE SHATTERS WALLS, opening new routes through the level. Beaten with HOIST-flung bodies, SLAM, or by baiting him into a wall: on wall impact he self-stuns 1.4s and takes 40 damage. That is the 'use the room' lesson, taught by one enemy with zero text. NOTE: because his charge mutates the tilemap at runtime, the pre-rendered level canvas must be patched - redraw ONLY the shattered tiles into the offscreen canvas (a handful of drawImage calls at the moment of impact), never rebuild the whole thing.

**Visual:** 28x34px, visibly bulkier than a guard. Riot plate #74828f, shield #515c68, glowing red visor #e02030. Charge telegraph = the body compresses 4px and 3 yellow #ffb43c chevrons appear ahead of him showing the charge lane.

**Stats:** hp 120, walk 68px/s, charge 520px/s for 1.1s, 30 damage, 700ms telegraph, wall impact self-stun 1.4s and 40 self-damage, immune to yank, spawn weight 6. Verified hits to kill: slam 4x, fling 5x - the environment is always the faster answer. First appears at level 5.

### debris (Severed body parts. Most of the payoff, and nearly free once they settle.)

**Comportamiento:** Gravity 620px/s^2, air drag 0.995 on vx, restitution 0.42 vertical / 0.6 horizontal (meat does not bounce like a ball; the asymmetry makes parts slide and skid, which reads as wet), spin damped 0.9 per bounce. REST RULE: when |vy| < 24 and grounded, snap the angle to the nearest of the 8 baked steps, set rest=true and STOP INTEGRATING - a resting limb costs one blit and zero math. Resting debris NEVER expires on a timer; it is freed only under pool pressure by evicting the OLDEST resting one (scan, free, THEN spawn - never cache an index across the free), so limbs stay on the floor for the whole level. BLOOD TRAIL BY DISTANCE, NOT TIME: while airborne and faster than 90px/s, trail += speed*dt, and every 14px emit one droplet and stamp one decal. Tying emission to distance stops a slow-tumbling arm machine-gunning droplets.

**Visual:** 6 pre-baked parts x 8 rotations = 48 tiny canvases, ~1,440px total, baked in under a millisecond at boot. Blit AXIS-ALIGNED with index = (round(ang*1.2732395) & 7) - NEVER ctx.rotate/save/restore per debris (a transformed blit costs 3-4x a plain one on Adreno and breaks integer pixel alignment). At 6-12px sprite sizes, 8 angular steps are visually indistinguishable from continuous rotation.

**Stats:** Pool cap 28. ~6 live in steady state after the rest-freeze, so ~22 frozen blits and 6 simulations. 3 tilemap probes per live debris per step = 18 probes/step. Spawn on kill: head + torso always, then each of 4 limbs with probability 0.55 + force*0.45, so a normal kill leaves a broken but mostly intact body and a big kill leaves six pieces - the variance is what stops it reading as a canned animation.

### bullet (A readable object in flight, never a hitscan.)

**Comportamiento:** Straight line at 300px/s along the guard's LOCKED vector, 3s life, freed on tile or player contact. Deliberately slow - a third of the player's swing peak - so it is dodgeable by moving perpendicular, which is guaranteed to work because the aim never tracks.

**Visual:** 4x4px core #ffb43c with a 2px #ff8c28 trail rect behind it, plus a 1px white leading pixel.

**Stats:** Pool cap 24, speed 300px/s, damage 22, life 3s.

## Progresion

A RUN IS 8 LEVELS AND THEN IT ENDS WITH AN ESCAPE - a win screen, not a fail state. All three existing games are endless-score games, so SYMBIOTE finishing gives the arcade a title you can actually BEAT.

SIZE RAMP (capped at depth 6, because past that larger levels only add walking and walking is not what makes this fun): W = 32+min(d,6)*2 -> 32,34,36,38,40,42,44,44. H = 72+min(d,6)*3 -> 72,75,78,81,84,87,88,88. Rooms 10->26. Pipes 2->5. Loop corridors 2->6.

ENEMY RAMP, each new threat getting one level to be learned by dying to it: scientists 4+d (4..11); guards d<1 ? 0 : min(6,d) (first at level 2); hazmat d<2 ? 0 : min(3,d-1) (first at level 3); turrets d<3 ? 0 : min(4,d-2) (first at level 4); enforcers d<4 ? 0 : min(2,d-4) (first at level 5).

WITHIN A LEVEL - a 4-beat curve driven by ONE integer, the ALERT counter 0-3, which drives spawn tables, lighting and music intensity together (which is why escalation feels authored despite being generated). BEAT 1, 0-25s, SLAUGHTER (alert 0): 6-9 scientists, 0-1 guards, no turrets, wide rooms with many anchors. Pure unopposed carnage - she is powerful BEFORE she is ever endangered, which is the correct order for a power fantasy. BEAT 2, 25-70s, RESISTANCE (alert 1): 4-6 scientists, 2-3 guards, 1 turret, 1 hazmat; corridors narrow so swinging needs shorter deliberate anchors. BEAT 3, 70-110s, LOCKDOWN (alert 2): alarm permanently on (SFX.alarm every 4s, a full-screen #c81028 rect at alpha 0.10-0.18 pulsing at ~2Hz), 2-4 scientists, 4-5 guards, 2 turrets, 1 enforcer, reinforcement waves of 2 every 20s capped at 6 living guards. BEAT 4, 110-150s, ESCAPE (alert 3): exit visible, 25s countdown, guards every 8s, but the room is a long open shaft ideal for swinging - the intended experience is one continuous high-speed swing to the exit while everything shoots at you.

ESCALATION: alert rises by 1 when an alarm is pressed, when a guard calls backup, OR when a beat's time threshold passes - whichever comes first, so a cautious player and a loud player converge within ~15 seconds. Alert never falls. This makes the alarm-pressing scientist genuinely matter (he costs ~15s of peace) without letting him ruin a run, and prevents the classic failure where a careful player experiences a flat boring level.

ANTI-CHEAP-DEATH: (a) no enemy spawns within 240px of the player or inside the camera rect; (b) after two hits within 2.0s, suppress all new AIM entries for 1.2s (the 'mercy beat' - invisible to her, she just feels like she got away with it; this is the specific mechanism that prevents the death spiral where one hit while swinging becomes four); (c) the first 8 seconds after a retry spawn no guard in the first room, so an instant retry is never instantly re-punished; (d) the max-2-concurrent-locks cap applies globally at every alert level.

SCORING, submitted ONCE at the end of the run so the leaderboard reads as one number: 100 per kill + 40 bonus per yank kill (rewards the signature verb) + 500 per level cleared + max(0, 90-seconds)*10 time bonus per level. Both speed and style pay.

## Juice

- HITSTOP: 5 frames normal kill / 8 frames dismemberment / 11 frames big kill / 6 frames player hit. Implemented as an internal S.freeze counter that skips physics while still consuming its dt - NEVER by returning early and expecting main.js's accumulator to stop. Never exceed 11 frames (183ms) or it reads as a dropped frame.
- SLOW-MOTION: only on dismemberment (scale 0.35 for 0.22s) and big kills (0.28 for 0.34s). Never on taking damage - slow-mo on damage feels like punishment. Implemented as sdt = dt*scale passed to every physics call, so the engine still ticks at 60Hz and input latency stays 16.7ms.
- SCREEN SHAKE via the engine's cam.shake(mag,dur): 3.5/0.16 normal kill, 5.5/0.24 dismemberment, 8.0/0.34 big kill, 6.0/0.20 player hit, 4.0/0.18 turret yank, 2.0/0.10 turret beam start, 6.0/0.30 death.
- WHITE FLASH: every enemy has a bakeFlash all-white frame shown for 120ms on hit. #ffffff is RESERVED for the hit flash and the font - lab walls use #f2f6f8 - so a white flash ALWAYS means impact and never competes with scenery.
- SCREEN FLASH: a full-screen white rect at alpha 0.045 for 45ms on a kill, 0.07 for 70ms on player damage. Two rects, no cost.
- PARTICLE COUNTS: 18 blood particles on a 2-piece tear, 26 on a 4-piece, 14 on a torso/head separation, 8 glass shards, 16 sparks on a turret yank, 40 on player death, 10 on a feed. Arterial spray is 6+force*4 per pulse across 3 pulses, capped at 26 per kill.
- SQUASH-STRETCH on the blob driven by speed, area-conserving (st=1+sq*0.45 along motion, 1/sqrt(st) across). Verified 60x56 at rest -> 87x47 at full stretch with area ratio exactly 1.000.
- TENTACLE TAUT/SLACK: when attached the limb draws 1px thinner with a brighter rim. Free feedback that teaches the hold-to-attach mechanic with zero tutorial.
- VIBRATION via input.js vibrate(): 28ms normal kill, [0,40,30,60] dismemberment, [0,50,40,90] big kill, 70ms player hit, 120ms death, 22ms lunge connect. The two-pulse array form reads as 'tear' where a single pulse reads as 'tap'.
- BAKED VIGNETTE: one offscreen canvas built at init with 10 coarse alpha bands, blitted once per frame. NEVER createRadialGradient - I grepped the codebase and there are ZERO gradients, shadowBlur or filters anywhere, which is exactly why the existing games hold 60fps; those are the classic Adreno-612-in-WebView frame killers.
- FLUORESCENT FLICKER: per light tile, a rect at alpha 0.06-0.14 driven by seeded rnd() so it stutters IRREGULARLY. A sine reads as 'pulsing'; irregular reads as 'failing'.
- ALARM STATE: full-screen #c81028 rect at alpha 0.10-0.18 pulsing ~2Hz, plus tile highlights swapped to alarm orange. Instantly readable as 'they know you are here'.
- PIPE DARKNESS: full-screen #04060a rect at alpha 0.82 with the blob drawn AFTER it, so only the symbiote and its rim glow remain visible - it reads as bioluminescent.

## Audio

- GORE.squelch (wet tear, the primary kill sound) = LAYERED: sfx({type:'noise',f0:900,f1:140,dur:0.22,vol:0.42}) plus sfx({type:'saw',f0:190,f1:55,dur:0.18,vol:0.20}). The low saw sweep is the 'body' and is what makes it read as flesh rather than static. Wet tissue = filtered noise sweeping DOWN, energy moving high to low as the tear opens.
- GORE.crunch (bone, on dismemberment) = sfx({type:'noise',f0:3200,f1:260,dur:0.09,vol:0.50}) plus sfx({type:'pulse',duty:0.125,f0:140,f1:60,dur:0.07,vol:0.30}). Bright noise transient plus a low pulse thump.
- GORE.snap (tendon, on a limb detaching) = sfx({type:'pulse',duty:0.125,f0:1400,f1:300,dur:0.045,vol:0.30}). Very short, very bright - this is the sound that sells separation.
- GORE.splat (debris hitting a surface at speed) = sfx({type:'noise',f0:600,f1:90,dur:0.13,vol:0.30}).
- GORE.drip (ambient, from the drip system) = sfx({type:'tri',f0:800,f1:400,dur:0.06,vol:0.10}).
- GORE.alarm2 (lockdown loop) = sfx({type:'saw',f0:330,f1:520,dur:0.30,vol:0.26}), fired every 4s at alert>=2.
- GORE.attach (tentacle bites wall) = sfx({type:'noise',f0:1600,f1:420,dur:0.06,vol:0.26}) plus sfx({type:'pulse',duty:0.25,f0:520,f1:900,dur:0.05,vol:0.18}).
- GORE.swingWhoosh (fired once per swing when tangential speed crosses 600px/s) = sfx({type:'noise',f0:400,f1:1800,dur:0.18,vol:0.16}). Rising noise tied to speed is what makes velocity audible.
- GORE.feed (heal on a yank kill) = sfx({type:'pulse',duty:0.25,f0:300,f1:760,dur:0.14,vol:0.26}) - a rising slurp, deliberately pitched so a chain of feeds forms an ascending phrase.
- CRITICAL VOICE BUDGET: audio.js has MAX_VOICES=12 and sfx() returns SILENTLY when exceeded. A dismemberment kill fires squelch+crunch+snap = 5 nodes. Verified: 1 gib kill = 5 nodes ok, 2 = 10 ok, 3 SIMULTANEOUS = 15 nodes -> OVERFLOW and sounds are dropped unpredictably, which manifests as 'gore sometimes sounds wrong' and is nearly impossible to diagnose later. FIX: a per-sound-type cooldown of 70ms in the module (S.sfxCool.squelch etc., decremented in update). 70ms both stays under the cap and matches the perceptual fusion window, so it improves the sound while fixing the budget.
- LAYER STAGGER: do NOT use setTimeout for the ~12ms offset between layers (the existing SFX presets use it, which is fine for menu sounds, but combat layers must stay locked to the hitstop). Use a small delayed micro-queue advanced in update().
- MUSIC: add a 'symbiote' entry to SONGS - bpm 144, three tracks. Bass tri pattern 'A...A...F...F...C...C...G...G...' vol 0.16; pulse duty 0.125 arpeggio 'a.c.e.c.a.c.e.c.f.a.c.a.g.b.d.b.' vol 0.06; noise 'H.hHH.h.H.hHH.h.' vol 0.06. The sequencer picks it up automatically from the meta.id - no other wiring needed.
